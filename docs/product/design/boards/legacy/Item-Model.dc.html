ROUND D · THE SYSTEM BEHIND TODAY
Items, facets and things made for a moment
Today is about to hold tasks, meetings, artifacts, requests, agent status and updates — and more kinds after that. So the durable work is not the screen, it is the contract every item on it obeys: one facet vocabulary with a ladder that says when a thing may become a chip, one item shape that makes adding a kind a matter of filling in a row, and one pattern for an artifact that exists for a moment.

Escalation. At most one per item, and only where rung 3 has already been true for a while and nothing happened. This is the loudest thing on the page and it is still not red.
carried 5+ days · blocking an agent
The hard cap: at most two chips above rung 1 on one item. A third collapses into +2 that expands on hover. This is the single rule that stops a page of ransom-note pills, and it is worth more than any individual chip design — because the failure mode of a facet vocabulary is never one bad chip, it is forty good ones at once.
The colour budget is unchanged. agent for provenance, accent for what is actionable or selected, the state tokens for exceptions. Priority, size, project, area, type and source are never coloured — not because they do not matter, but because they always apply, and a facet that is always present cannot also be a signal.

ONE ITEM CONTRACT — ADDING A KIND IS FILLING IN A ROW
Everything on the spine is an item, and an item is five things: a kind (which picks the glyph and the lead affordance), a moment (a time, a gap, or none — which sends it to the rail), a title, its facets from the one vocabulary above, and its verbs. Add a kind by declaring those. Nothing else in the screen changes, which is the whole point of writing it down now rather than after the fourth kind.

The standup was never a copy button — it is an artifact a scheduled run produced, bound to an obligation on the calendar. Once that is the shape, the pattern generalises: an agenda before a meeting, a pre-read before a review, a weekly summary before Friday. Same card, same four parts — what it is · what made it · what it is for · how fresh it is relative to that.
Freshness here is not the usual staleness. Everywhere else stale means the screen is behind the data. Here it means the artifact is behind its own occasion — a standup written at 06:02 for a 09:15 standup, where two things changed at 08:40, is wrong in a way that matters in twenty-five minutes. That is a genuinely different measurement and it needs the binding to compute at all.
Verbs are Open · Copy · Ask for changes, and Copy's menu never contains a send. Metistry does not post, and the pattern must not quietly introduce posting on the fourth artifact type when nobody is looking. That is why the rule lives on the pattern and not on the standup card.

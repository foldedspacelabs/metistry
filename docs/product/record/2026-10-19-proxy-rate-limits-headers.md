- 2026-10-19 — **The console's proxy listener can now stand on the internet: a stranger is bounded in how often it is answered, and never in what it is told.** Every way a phone will reach a Mac from outside puts the sign-in page where any scanner can find it, so the listener a provider forwards to now rate-limits every answer it gives without a session — the shell, the liveness and identity reads, the passkey ceremonies, a wrong host, and every refusal of a guessed credential — per client address and under a ceiling for the whole listener, answering `429` with a `Retry-After` and writing one audit row per refused burst rather than one per probe, so the record shows the attack and is not filled by it. The client's address is the one the provider itself wrote into its forwarding header, read on that listener alone and used for limiting alone: no credential decision anywhere reads a forwarded header, so a header can slow a caller but can never raise one, and a request a real credential admits is never limited — the owner's phone keeps working on an address a scanner shares with it. Every answer there also carries the headers a public origin owes a browser: strict transport, a content-security policy written for the shell as it is, no content sniffing, no referrer, no framing. The main listener on the Mac is untouched by all of it, and there is no configuration that turns any of it off.
